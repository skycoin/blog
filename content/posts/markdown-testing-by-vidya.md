# The crypto-Jedi’s guide to weaning yourself from coinmarketcap.com or, how to view Skycoin through an untainted lense.

One of the first things a neophyte crypto-enthusiast learns is to check the standing of a coin on Coinmarketcap. The idea that a high ranking on Coinmarketcap is the sole metric by which to measure the success of a coin is so embedded in the collective crypto-unconscious that it reveals itself in our conversations. How many times have you heard the term ‘Top 100 coin’ ? The implicit perception in that phrase is that; firstly, Coinmarketcap is referenced as the sole authority and secondly, that marketcap as calculated by CMC is the definitive metric. This reasoning is fatally flawed.

Before we proceed, I would like to reiterate that this essay in no way compromises [my position ](https://medium.com/skyfleet-captains-log/why-it-matters-that-skycoin-is-the-sole-remaining-anti-establishment-coin-975546a5e580)on using fiat as a metric with which to value cryptocurrency. Ultimately we will have to discard the absurd notion that the value of crypto lies in is it’s ability to earn vast amounts of fiat but for now we are caught between a rock and a hard place. Despite it’s fiat value, crypto has very little purchasing power in the real world so, apart from becoming ascetics, we have little choice other than to partake in the crypto:fiat exchange game. How then do we break free from the single dimensional marketcap metric and gain a deeper insight into the current and future potential fiat value of a coin in order to make good investment decisions?

As a valuation metric, marketcap does seem like a logical choice at first. What can be simpler than multiplying the total supply of a coin with the current mean average across exchanges to see which coin is worth the most? This is after all how the stock market has been doing things for years. Makes sense doesn’t it? It does, until you start scratching a bit deeper.

In their most excellent [blogpost](https://blog.nomics.com/essays/crypto-market-cap-review-emerging-alternatives/#calc) at nomics.com, Nathaniel Whittemore and Clay Collins constructed a four point critique of using marketcap as a measure, proposes improved methods of using marketcap-style evaluations and finally, proposes three alternatives to marketcap as value assessments.

In a nutshell, their four critiques;

* **Tokens != stocks.** Stocks gives the owner all kinds of shareholder rights such as disclosure of information. It also gives the owner claims to future profits or benefits from a liquidity events such as a public listing. Token or coin ownership on the other hand affords the holder none of the above. Token price is not calculated based on projected real value profits but rather on speculation. The point here is that a metric that works for one thing does not necessarily work for another.
* **Inflation schedules.** When it comes to equity, The amount of stock that is issued can only be increased through a stock split but in general, the number is fixed. Cryptos tend to have a continuous injection of new units into the available supply. To calculate a simple marketcap, two variables are used, ie available supply and price. With stocks, the supply variable is static and only the price variable changes. Crypto on the other hand uses two moving variables and this is problematic because a rising marketcap in this case might just mean that there is more of the coin available and doesn’t necessarily reflect it’s true performance.
* **The circulating supply challenge.** CMC and other ranking sites use circulating supply to calculate marketcap in an attempt to be more accurate. However, circulating supply can not possibly account for lost coins. An [estimated ](https://blog.chainalysis.com/reports/money-supply)2.3 to 4 million Bitcoins are irrecoverably lost. If this number was calculated into the circulating supply, it would impact Bitcoin’s marketcap by as much as 25%.
* **Redemption Impact.** This is arguably the most pertinent point of the critique. Essentially, redemption impact measures a cryptocurrency’s true real world value by calculating the impact that a large buy or sell order would have on the asset price. Redemption Impact Score (RIS) is calculated according to a [formula](https://twitter.com/gaborgurbacs/status/1049097000283770880) put forward by ETF specialist Gabor Gurbacs. Assets with a low RIS score will be able to sustain it’s value during a large redemption while assets with a high RIS score won;t be able to do so. Most cryptocurrencies have a high RIS and are unable to sustain their price against a dump which gives credence to the statement that marketcap is a theoretical construct that probably does more to obfuscate rather than illuminate the true value and potential of a coin.

I will not explore improvements on marketcap evaluation methods in this piece as I consider it to be a moot point but will move on directly to alternatives to marketcap as value assessments.

### Network Value to Transaction Ratio (NVT)

NVT is measured as \[Price x Supply\] / Transaction count. NVT indicates the strength of a cryptocurrency as a payment network. A high NVT means that the valuation of the network is overshooting the actual value being exchanged on the network. When the NVT is low, the situation is inverted and the network is being undervalued.

Development activity

Popular ranking site, coingeko, allows users to arrange coins according to several different metrics other than marketcap. Among these metrics is development activity. Coingeko tracks the public code repo’s of open source cryptocurrency projects and measures the amount of activity such as commits, issues closed and forks among others to afford us a view into just how actively a given cryptocurrency is being developed. This is important why? Because, a coin might have a high CMC ranking at the moment but, if we see that development is starting to stagnate, that gives an early warning that the coin might not remain competitive much longer and that it could be a good idea to empty those bags.

Let’s take a look at how this works. At the time of publication, Skycoin was ranked 215 on CMC and 190 on Coingeko. The sites use slightly different calculation algorithms so the result is invariably different, strengthening my case that marketcap is a terrible valuation metric.

In terms of active development, Skycoin outperforms several so called top 100 coins including HOLO, a coin sometimes mistakenly positioned as a Skycoin competitor with a much higher marketcap rating at number 40.

Contributor to commit ratio

This is a refinement on the developer ratio used by Coingeko and is calculated by simply dividing the number of commits by the number of listed contributors to obtain an idea of how active the contributors are. The reasoning here is that many contributors might have stopped committing code altogether but are still counted. This leads to a skewed outcome when calculating the development activity metric.

Skycoin is the sole asset to show a positive contributor to commit ratio indicating that Skycoin’s developers outperform the top 3 coins developers in terms of activity. One might argue that top coins don’t require much more development since they are already ‘successful’ but that would be flawed logic. Both Ethereum and Bitcoin suffer tremendous scaling issues and both are claiming to be solving them. If this was the case, one would expect to see a hive of activity on those repos but that is not the case. Ethereum development appears to be virtually stagnant. Also bear in mind that Skycoin has been in development since 2012 on Github and the first code was written in 2011. Instead of slowing down, Skycoin development activity seems to be increasing. To me this is a sign of a long term project that has gained a lot of traction and is undervalued right now. Super green flag for a hodler like myself.
